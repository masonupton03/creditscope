    How these numbers are computed, and what they assume

    Time-weighted return (the number to compare with SPY)

    The portfolio is valued at every close. Each day’s return strips out that day’s deposits and withdrawals, then the daily returns are chained. Flows are assumed to happen at the close, at the price you traded. This measures how the investments performed, regardless of when money moved.

    rt = (Valuet − Flowt) ÷ Valuet−1 − 1

    Existing holdings and outside funding

    Enter an existing position as a buy with its real purchase date and price and tick funded from outside. Its cost (plus fee) is then treated as a deposit on that date, so it is not mistaken for performance: the time-weighted return is unaffected by it, the dollar “net contributions” include it, and Modified Dietz weights it by how long it has been invested. A buy that is not marked outside is paid from cash in the ledger (record a deposit first) and warns if cash would go negative. Sales work the same way in reverse. History before your earliest transaction is simply not measured.

    Dividends and reinvestment

    Dividends are credited to cash on the ex-date, for the shares held at the previous close, unless you record your own dividend transactions (then the automatic ones for that security are switched off). To model reinvestment, record the dividend and a buy with reinvested ticked: use “Dividend + reinvestment”. Nothing is reinvested automatically. The SPY comparison is labelled on every number and you can choose how SPY treats its own dividends: held as cash (matches a portfolio that keeps them), reinvested on the ex-date (matches one that reinvests), or price only. “Automatic” follows what you recorded.

    Dates: actual versus applied

    Each transaction keeps the date you entered, even a weekend or holiday. Calculations apply it at the close of the first trading day on or after that date; the “Applied on” column shows it. The calendar does not distinguish holidays from weekends, so a holiday is simply the next date with a price.

    Timing is an approximation

    Deposits, withdrawals and trades are assumed to happen at the end of the day at your traded price. Real orders fill at intraday times and dividends are paid days after the ex-date, so very short-period returns around large flows are approximate. The approximation does not accumulate: it only affects the day of each flow.

    Change in account value is not a return

    Depositing $1,000 raises the balance by $1,000 and earns nothing. The page therefore shows value change, net contributions and investment gain (value change minus contributions, in dollars) next to the percentage returns.

    Modified Dietz

    A dollar-weighted estimate that weights each flow by the fraction of the period it was invested; it reflects the timing of your deposits, so it can differ from the time-weighted figure.

    R = (V₁ − V₀ − ΣF) ÷ (V₀ + Σ w·F), w = (T − t) ÷ T

    Dividends, splits and missing data

    Dividends are credited to cash on the ex-date for the shares held at the previous close and are not reinvested. Share counts are adjusted for later stock splits. If a security’s dividend history is unknown, both the portfolio and SPY switch to price return and say so. A missing price is carried forward and flagged; holdings are never valued at cost to fill a gap, and performance starts after the last day a price was missing.

    Cost basis and gains

    First-in, first-out lots; buy fees are part of cost, sell fees reduce proceeds. Taxes, wash sales, currencies, options and short positions are not modelled.

    Risk

    Volatility is the sample standard deviation of daily time-weighted returns × √252 (20 returns needed). Beta and correlation use overlapping daily returns with SPY (60 needed). Maximum drawdown is measured on the time-weighted index. Concentration uses the weights of positions only: top holdings, the Herfindahl index (sum of squared weights) and its inverse, the effective number of equal-sized holdings.

    Privacy

    On the public site this page only reads a simulated sample. In the personal edition your ledger is stored in a file outside the code repository, served to localhost only, never put in browser storage or the offline cache, and no brokerage is connected. Educational tool, not investment advice.

    CreditScope is a class project. Nothing on this page is investment advice; past performance does not predict future results.
