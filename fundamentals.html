    Where this data comes from, how ratios are computed, and the limits

    Source and reuse

    Figures come from the SEC’s XBRL “company facts” data (data.sec.gov), which companies file with their annual reports. The SEC states that information on sec.gov is public and may be copied and distributed without its permission, and asks for citation; this site cites the SEC and links each figure to its filing. No account, API key or personal data is needed to view anything.

    What is included

    Annual (10-K) figures from the standard us-gaap tags, up to twelve fiscal years. Quarterly reports, non-standard (company-specific) tags and foreign filers that use IFRS are not included. The tag that supplied each number is stored with it; when a company changed tags over time, the first tag in a fixed priority list that has a value for the year is used.

    Restatements and fiscal years

    If a period was reported more than once (a restatement or a comparative in a later report) the latest filed value is shown and linked. A fiscal year is labelled by the year its period ends; a 52/53-week year ending in the first week of January is labelled with the previous year.

    Ratios

    Revenue growth = Revenuet ÷ Revenuet−1 − 1 · Margin = metric ÷ Revenue · FCF = Operating cash flow − Capital expenditure
    ROE = Net income ÷ average equity · ROA = Net income ÷ average assets · Debt-to-equity = Debt ÷ Equity · CAGR = (Revenueend ÷ Revenuestart)1/years − 1

    A ratio is left blank (never zero) when a number it needs is missing or its denominator is not positive, for example return on equity when equity is negative. Gross profit is revenue minus cost of revenue when no total was reported, and is marked as derived. “Debt” is long-term debt including its current portion where the company reports it.

    Freshness

    Each company shows the period end of its latest annual report, the date that report was filed, and when this data was compiled. A warning appears when the latest report is more than about fifteen months old. Data is a snapshot, not live.

    Limits

    Reported figures are not adjusted for inflation, acquisitions, accounting changes or one-off items. Companies differ in how they use tags, so cross-company comparisons need care. Educational tool, not investment advice.
