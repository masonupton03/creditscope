          How the comparison works

          One window, one basis

          Every security is measured from the latest of their individual period starts to the earliest of their last dates, and only on dates when all of them traded. A newly listed security therefore shortens the window for everyone, and the notice says so. If any security lacks dividend history, all returns switch to price return (dividends excluded) rather than mixing total and price returns.

          Metrics

          Growth of 100 rebases every security to 100 at the window start. Volatility is the sample standard deviation of daily returns × √252 (20 returns needed). Maximum drawdown is the largest fall from a running peak. Beta is the covariance of a security’s daily returns with SPY’s divided by SPY’s variance, and correlation is the correlation coefficient; both, and the correlation matrix, need at least 60 shared returns. These are historical statistics, not forecasts.

          Fundamentals

          Company figures come from annual reports (see the Fundamentals page for sources, definitions and limits). Ratios are blank when not meaningful. Companies with negative equity, different fiscal year ends or different accounting tags are not strictly comparable; compare the trends, not just single numbers.

          Real versus simulated

          The public site uses simulated prices and fictional companies, each labelled. In the private personal edition on the owner’s computer the price section can use real, licensed-for-private-use data, which is never published.
