## Assessment

**Typography.** One system sans stack, a short scale (24 px page title, 15–16 px section titles, 14.5 px body, 12–13 px labels),
tabular figures so columns of numbers align, sentence-case labels and a true minus sign. Titles, key figures and labels read as
three clear levels on every page. Nothing is set in capitals or monospace any more. Verdict: consistent and calm.

**Spacing.** An 8-point scale with generous space between sections and hairlines instead of boxes. Key figures are one flat strip.
The earlier problems (ticker header crowded under the "Try" chips on Fundamentals, a gray fill in wrapped stat strips) are fixed.
Verdict: consistent.

**Chart readability.** Subject in blue, benchmark in neutral grey, other series from the validated categorical order, 2 px lines,
a recessive grid, horizontal axis labels, at most six value ticks, a small legend marker. Redundant axis titles were removed (the
chart title or caption already states the unit). Drawdown and volume charts are quiet and legible. Verdict: good; the busiest chart
is the three-line drawdown in Compare, where lines overlap by nature.

**Visual hierarchy.** Each page has one focus: Today the latest values, Explorer the chart and four key figures, Fundamentals the
five-line headline and four charts, Compare the common-window table, Portfolio the performance chart. Secondary material (More
metrics, risk detail, reconciliation, transactions, methodology) is behind a quiet expander. Amber appears only where data is
simulated or a caution applies, so it carries meaning.

**Navigation.** Markets / Research / Portfolio on one bar, the section's pages as tabs below, About as a small link. Every page
tells you where you are (current area highlighted, current tab underlined) and cross-page links keep the ticker.

## Changes made during this review

- Light theme is the first-visit default; the saved preference is applied in `<head>` so there is no flash, and only an explicit
  toggle is stored.
- Footers no longer say "is a class project" (the rubric does not require it; it undercut the product feel). The byline "A Financial
  Data Analytics project by Mason Upton" remains on the three assignment pages.
- Removed repeated y-axis titles; x labels no longer rotate 45° on Fundamentals; legend markers are the same small size on every chart.
- Compare tables now sit in a panel so header and row cells share a background; row headers are styled as body text.
- Concentration figures on Portfolio are a tidy 2 × 2; the Compare captions are shorter; Fundamentals has proper spacing under search.

## Remaining design concerns (none blocks launch)

1. **First-run emptiness.** Today and Trends show dashes and single-point charts until more daily runs exist. That is data, not
   design, and it fixes itself by Dec 3.
2. **Trends on a phone is long** (about 5,000 px). Accepted: the assignment requires four full findings with explanations.
3. **Fundamentals tables are 10 years wide.** They scroll horizontally with a sticky first column; the last visible column is cut
   off at the edge as the scroll cue. Showing fewer years by default on narrow screens is an option.
4. **Demo-set artefacts.** Because the fictional demo companies share no tickers with the simulated securities, cross-links show
   "n/a" for almost every ticker, and the Compare page carries two "simulated" notices. Real SEC data will resolve this.
5. **Quiet side columns.** The Explorer watchlist column is mostly empty text until a visitor saves a security.
6. **Stacked expanders on Portfolio** (three in a row) read as a list of links; fine for analysts, slightly sparse for a quick look.
7. **Hand-checked on three browsers?** No: only Chrome was exercised. Safari and Firefox, and a real phone, are still unchecked.
